One ladder, three hues

A board type is a whole scene, not a rung on one. Idea boards take a deep hunter green, Note boards a deep violet; To-Do boards keep the water blue. Each is the same ladder — every rung reproduces its WCAG relative luminance to 4dp, and only the family's hue moves. (OKLCH lightness and chroma are the aiming coordinates; the callout at the foot says why they could not also be pinned, and the table prints what they actually land on.) Read the three sheets against each other: the band's fall, the vignette, the dither, the note frames and the title compartment all carry the hue, because the tokens are rebound rather than the backgrounds overridden.

Rob asked for new backgrounds for the Idea and Note boards, with the To-Do boards keeping the ink well blue. The derivation is hue and only hue: each rung converted to OKLCH, its hue moved, and the result re-solved against sRGB so the WCAG luminance reproduces the To-Do rung's. That is why the verification table above needed no new rows for §2.3, §2.5 or §2.7 — those ratios are functions of luminance, and none of it moved. The four surface layers carry the hue because the token names are rebound, not the backgrounds overridden: a new --board-bg token would have recoloured the fill and left blue furniture standing on a green ground.

Two things this render is the record for. OKLCH lightness and chroma could not also be pinned — OKLab lightness and WCAG luminance are different functions of the same colour, so a hue rotation cannot hold both. Luminance won, because it is what the ratios are made of; L lands within 0.010 and C within 0.007, and the table above prints the residuals rather than hiding them. And the Idea ladder's --card (C 0.043 against the blue's 0.050) and --deep (0.025 against 0.027) sit at the sRGB maximum for their luminance and hue — sRGB's blue primary is dark enough to buy chroma almost free, green's carries 0.7152 of the luminance and cannot. A greener dark card at that value does not exist.
