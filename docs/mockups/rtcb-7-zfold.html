Rolling Temporal Calendar Board — unfolded Z Fold / dual-pane

Two viewports, two panes: board left, week right

On the unfolded Z Fold the calendar doesn't float over the board — it takes the entire right half, full height, always visible: the device is two viewports side by side, so each half is a complete pane. The left half is the board with its boards rail; the right half is the 7-day rolling stack at full height — today lit and largest at the top, the week receding beneath it. The small corner control folds the calendar back to the right edge rail if the person wants the board full-width; unfolding it again restores the pane. Same 7-day law on every viewport: today at top, always, rolling at midnight.

Why the fold gets the whole half

  • Two viewports side by side — a one-third overlay would waste the pane. The calendar is a first-class citizen at full height: seven whole day cards, today's visible without tapping anything.
  • The crease is the seam between doing (left) and when (right). The board's geometry never reflows — its logical sheet just renders in the left viewport, exactly as B32's viewport-derived law already does.
  • The corner control folds the calendar to the collapsed right-edge rail (mockup 6) when full-width board focus is wanted; it re-expands to the half-pane. Collapse is always available; the pane is the default on fold.
  • Same stack, same laws: today lit and largest at top, tomorrow near, then far; yesterday already rolled into the archive at midnight. Nothing new to learn moving between phone, fold, and desktop.
